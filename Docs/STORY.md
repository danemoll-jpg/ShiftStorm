# ShiftStorm — Story Direction (Dan, 2026-11-15)

Source: Dan's `ShiftStorm Story Direction.docx` (claude.ai project "Transformation Racing Concept"). This is the story
the game follows. **Track lists are ideas, not commitments** (Dan: "we will likely use some but some we won't and others
may be added as ideas come"). This supersedes the track list in the original concept doc (Clockwork Cascade, Cloud
Orchard, Lantern Reef are now just more ideas in the pool).

## Premise
A whimsical arcade racer in the Tee'd Off universe with vehicles that drive, boat, fly and dive. A **story mode** is
planned from the start so characters, tracks and events fit together — the plot gives a reason for the adventure without
limiting fun, imaginative courses.

A charismatic former stunt performer organises the spectacular racing tour he always wished existed. His idea of
"perfectly safe" makes everyone nervous, and he gets more ambitious as the tour succeeds. **His arc:** a great event
doesn't need to keep topping the last stunt — the racers, friendships and places matter more.

## The organiser — Buck "Big Finish" Barlow (working name; name and look are proposals)
- Warm, enthusiastic, sincerely sure his extraordinary plans will be wonderful; competent enough to be believable, but
  always underestimates how scary his plans sound.
- Voice: inspired by Bullwinkle's qualities — rounded, genial, unhurried, cheerfully confident, funny emphasis — but its
  own identity.
- Calm about outrageous things: *"Oh, no. We're not jumping over the Ferris wheel. That'd be irresponsible. We're going
  through it."*
- Motivation: fears people stopped finding him exciting; the tour is his comeback, early success pushes him to escalate.
  Realisation: people already enjoy what they're building together.
- Look (to explore): barrel-chested older showman, impressive mustache, faded stunt jacket, bright scarf, big smile; human
  or animal (broad bear or stocky dog).
- Running jokes: he gives the safety briefing while someone behind him quietly corrects the signs; exaggerated anecdotes
  (*"I used to jump six buses right over there. Seven, according to the posters. We counted the snack wagon."*).
- **ElevenLabs voice: "ShiftStorm Buck"** (made by Dan, 2026-11-15) — use this voice for every Buck line.
- **Voice prompt (ElevenLabs Voice Design, 2026-11-15; Dan makes the voice, then tells Code its name):**
  *"A warm, booming, barrel-chested older American man in his sixties, a retired stunt performer turned showman. Rounded,
  genial, slightly dopey-sweet tone with a big friendly chest resonance. Unhurried, easygoing delivery with cheerful,
  unshakable confidence, as if nothing could possibly go wrong. Playful comic emphasis that lands on odd words, drawn-out
  vowels, and a little proud chuckle. Clear, theatrical announcer projection, but relaxed and folksy rather than loud.

  Preview text: *"Welcome, welcome, one and all! Now, I want to assure everybody this event is perfectly safe. Oh, no.
  We're not jumping over the Ferris wheel. That'd be irresponsible. We're going through it. I used to jump six buses right

- Note: **Barnaby** (always called by his name, Dan 2026-11-16) stays the race caller (narrates races); Buck is the
  organiser (story, briefings, cutscenes).

## The tracks so far
- **Paradise Plunge — the private demonstration** (built). Buck's "gentle introductory outing": a private trial of the
  vehicles that tests all four modes and introduces the cast. He checked the ramps, underestimated the wildlife: *"The big
  fellow's a plant eater. Lovely disposition. Now, the other fellow — let's keep moving."* Its success gives him the
  confidence to go public.
- **Coaster Coast — the public debut** (Round 10). The tour's opening night and Buck's return to the spotlight. Possible
  link: he once did stunt shows at this park, knows the staff, persuaded the owner. Crowds and fireworks vs. Paradise
  Plunge's small private test — now he has expectations to live up to.

- **Big Top Breakdown — the dare** (Round 15, the Tour's third stop, "Growing success"). The tour has momentum; an old show-business
  acquaintance wants a piece of the spotlight. Answers to the four questions:
  - *Fun and distinctive to race:* a big-top circus camp in bright, gusty daylight (Coaster Coast is a seaside fair at night):
    weave down a midway between rolling circus wagons, a clown-car pile-up jump with a choice (ride the Globe of Death, a loop
    inside a steel-mesh sphere, or open the wings and fly past it), once round the ring under the big top in front of the crowd,
    then fired out of a giant cannon as a human cannonball through hoops under the canvas and out of the top flap, a long flight
    over the camp to the river (elephants, a paddle-steamer show boat, a rocky rapids shortcut), and down into the lake past the
    escape artist's tank and through a sunken circus wagon, back up into the ring's splash pool.
  - *Why Buck brings the tour here:* **Lionel Lustre**, ringmaster of the Lustre Brothers Circus, shared a bill with Buck in 1987
    (Lionel had elephants; Buck had a bus). He has heard Buck "went respectable" and dares him in a letter to prove he still has
    it. Buck can't refuse a dare, or a crowd.
  - *Who cares about this place:* Lionel and his performers (the clowns, the strongman, the trapeze family, the escape artist):
    a travelling show that is a bit ramshackle ("Breakdown": wagons roll where they like, the top flap blew open, the cannon is
    "mostly confetti") but proud of it.
  - *What changes:* Buck and Lionel go from rivals to old friends who admit they both still love the show: Lionel shakes his hand
    ("we still have it"; Buck chooses to hear the "we"). The player's challenge here is **Vivi's** (her Cannonball Run: the
    cannon is "her stage"), the first time Vivi steps forward as a rival.
  - *Lionel's voice (ElevenLabs Voice Design prompt, for Dan; until then Buck reads Lionel's letter aloud):* "A theatrical older
    British ringmaster in his sixties: a rich, booming, plummy baritone with grand rolling R's and dramatic pauses, smooth and a
    little vain, warmly mischievous rather than villainous, perfect clear diction, speaking as if to a packed big top." Name it
    "ShiftStorm Lionel". His look: `Docs/MODELS-NEEDED.md` (Round 15).

## Choosing tracks
**Pick tracks because they'd be fun to race.** The tour connects them — no real geography, shared theme or open world.
Each stop needs only a small purpose: introduce a host, develop a rivalry, expose an extravagant promise, or let someone
help. Buck's reasons can be flimsy (*"They offered us exclusive use of the facilities." "Buck, this is an active
volcano." "Well, yes. That's why the weekend was available."*).

Before committing to a new track, answer: What makes it fun and distinctive to race? Why has Buck brought the tour here?
Who cares about this place? What changes for the characters during this stop?

## Escalation across the campaign
| Stage | Story | Tracks |
|---|---|---|
| The trial | Buck demonstrates the idea, recruits the first racers | Paradise Plunge: all four modes |
| The launch | The tour meets its audience; Buck has something to prove | Coaster Coast: public spectacle |
| Growing success | Confidence, rivalries, expectations grow; bigger promises | More unusual venues and routes |
| Going too far | Outdoing himself causes problems for people he cares about | Outrageous combinations, complications |
| The finale | Buck accepts help and shares responsibility | A spectacular final race built through cooperation |
Escalation varies in kind — not every course bigger (a cramped haunted hotel that rearranges itself can feel wilder than
a huge outdoor course). The finale stays spectacular; Buck grows by listening, not by giving up showmanship.

## Supporting cast and conflict
Mix returning Tee'd Off characters with new racers and hosts. Useful new roles: **a practical event coordinator** (turns
Buck's promises into workable plans; learns when to trust him), **a recurring rival racer** (relationship with the player
develops, maybe becomes an ally), **local hosts** who care about their venues. An ambitious sponsor / promoter is
optional; no conventional villain is required — Buck's promises, rivalries and expectations are enough.

## Story mode principles
- Accessible to people who never played Tee'd Off; rewarding for those who did (personalities, voices, places, callbacks).
- Progress mainly by qualifying / championship points, not winning every race.
- Optional rival challenges and practice events reuse tracks to develop relationships without many bespoke missions.
- Core: a joyful racing adventure that gets more outrageous, held together by a likable showman learning that the people
  taking part make his grand tour worth doing.
